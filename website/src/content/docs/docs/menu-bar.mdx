---
title: Menu Bar Badge
description: tili-menubar, the NSStatusItem workspace badge that ships alongside the daemon.
---

`tili start` also installs and starts `tili-menubar` alongside the daemon —
a small `NSStatusItem` badge showing the active workspace on your focused
monitor.

## What it shows

- The badge title is the focused monitor's active workspace name — the
  unambiguous "current workspace" for a single badge, since more than one
  monitor's workspace can independently be "active" at once.
- The dropdown menu lists every workspace (a `•` prefix marks the current
  one), then **Open Settings** and **Quit tili**.
- It hides itself entirely — not a stale badge, nothing — whenever the
  daemon isn't reachable, including when it's stopped from outside
  tili-menubar entirely (e.g. `tili stop` from the CLI).

## Menu actions

| Item | Behavior |
|---|---|
| A workspace name | Switches the active workspace to it. |
| **Open Settings** | Opens `~/.config/tili/tili.kdl`, trying `$EDITOR` first (if set), then `open <path>` (Launch Services' registered default for `.kdl`), then `open -a TextEdit <path>` as a last resort that's guaranteed to work. |
| **Quit tili** | Runs `tili stop` (stopping the daemon too, not just this process) and then exits. |

## Staying in sync

Under the hood it stays in sync via a server-side long-poll
(`Command::WaitForChange`), not polling — consistent with the rest of
tili's event-driven design. The background thread blocks on that call
(zero CPU while idle) and only does real work once it unblocks, either
because something actually changed or the daemon's own internal wait hit
its 30s idle timeout — both look the same from the client's side by
design.

If the daemon becomes unreachable, tili-menubar hides the badge
immediately and retries once a second. After roughly a minute of
consecutive failures (60 attempts), it gives up, unloads its own
LaunchAgent, and stops itself — treating a daemon that's been gone that
long as "stopped on purpose" (e.g. via `tili stop`) rather than a
transient blip like the brief restart a Homebrew upgrade can trigger.

It starts/stops/uninstalls automatically with `tili start`/`stop`/
`uninstall`, so there's nothing extra to run yourself.
